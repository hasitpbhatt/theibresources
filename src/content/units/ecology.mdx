## Populations

### Growth

**Exponential growth** assumes unlimited resources: dN/dt = rN. It describes the early phase of
colonisation and nothing else for long.

**Logistic growth** adds a carrying capacity K: dN/dt = rN(K − N)/K. Growth is fastest at N = K/2.
Logistic curves are a useful approximation, not a law — real populations overshoot, oscillate and
crash.

### Limiting factors

- **Density-dependent** — competition, disease, predation. Effect strengthens as density rises,
  which is what produces regulation.
- **Density-independent** — weather, fire, catastrophe. Effect is unrelated to density, so these
  cannot regulate a population, only reduce it.

### Estimating population size

- **Lincoln index** (mark-recapture): N = (M × C) / R, where M is marked, C is the second sample,
  R is recaptures. Assumes a closed population, equal catchability, and no mortality or marking
  effect.
- **Quadrat sampling** — abundance and percentage cover for sessile organisms.
- **Transects** — how composition changes across a gradient.

Know the assumptions of the Lincoln index; exam questions test them repeatedly because the
assumptions are exactly where field data breaks.

## Communities and interactions

### Types of interaction

| Interaction | Effect on A | Effect on B |
|---|---|---|
| Competition | − | − |
| Predation | + | − |
| Herbivory | + | − |
| Parasitism | + | − |
| Mutualism | + | + |
| Commensalism | + | 0 |
| Amensalism | − | 0 |

### Competition

**Interspecific competition** drives **competitive exclusion** — two species cannot occupy the
same niche indefinitely. **Resource partitioning** is how coexistence happens: species divide the
niche by time, space, or food type.

**Fundamental niche** is the full range a species could occupy without competitors; **realised
niche** is what it actually occupies. The difference is measured in competitors.

### Predator–prey dynamics

Classic coupled oscillation: prey increase, predators increase, prey crash, predators crash, prey
recover. In reality the cycles are damped or obscured by other factors, but the mechanism is real
and worth being able to derive.

**Keystone species** have disproportionate influence relative to their abundance. Remove one and
the community reorganises — often a trophic cascade.

## Energy flow and nutrient cycling

### Trophic levels

Sun → producer → primary consumer → secondary → tertiary. Roughly 10% of energy transfers between
levels; the rest is lost as heat through respiration, or remains undigested.

That 10% is why food chains are short. It is arithmetic, not convention: a fourth trophic level
receives 0.1% of the original energy.

### Productivity

- **Gross primary productivity (GPP)** — total energy fixed by photosynthesis.
- **Net primary productivity (NPP)** — GPP minus respiratory losses. This is what is available to
  consumers.
- **Secondary productivity** — energy incorporated by consumers.

NPP varies with light, water, temperature and nutrient availability. Tropical rainforests and
estuaries are highest; deserts and open ocean are lowest.

### Nutrient cycles

Unlike energy, matter cycles. Two archetypes:

- **Gaseous** (carbon, nitrogen) — large atmospheric reservoir, fast turnover, globally mixed.
- **Sedimentary** (phosphorus, sulphur) — small atmospheric component, slow, local.

Phosphorus has no gaseous phase, which is why it is usually the limiting nutrient in freshwater
and why it is a non-renewable resource in practice.

### The nitrogen cycle

Fixation (biological, lightning, industrial Haber–Bosch) → ammonification → nitrification →
assimilation → denitrification.

The Haber–Bosch process now fixes more nitrogen than all natural processes combined. That single
industrial reaction feeds roughly half the world and is responsible for a large share of
eutrophication and N₂O emissions. It is the clearest example of a technology whose benefits and
harms come from the same mechanism.

## Natural selection

### The mechanism, stated precisely

1. **Variation** exists within a population.
2. Variation is **heritable**.
3. More offspring are produced than can survive.
4. Individuals with favourable variations **survive and reproduce** more.
5. Allele frequencies **shift** in the next generation.

Every step is required. Remove heritability and it is not evolution; remove differential
reproduction and nothing changes.

### Types

- **Directional** — one extreme favoured.
- **Stabilising** — the middle favoured, variance reduced.
- **Disruptive** — both extremes favoured, bimodal distribution.

### Evidence

- **Fossil record** — transitional forms, stratigraphic ordering.
- **Homologous structures** — same origin, different function (forelimbs).
- **Analogous structures** — different origin, same function (bird and insect wings) — evidence of
  convergent selection, not common descent.
- **Molecular** — shared genetic code, conserved sequences, phylogenetic congruence.
- **Observed** — antibiotic resistance, industrial melanism, Darwin's finches, stickleback
  armour, beak size in Galápagos finches measured generation by generation.

The last category is the strongest, because it is observed rather than inferred.

### Hardy–Weinberg

p² + 2pq + q² = 1 for two alleles at one locus. Describes allele frequencies in a population with
no selection, no mutation, no migration, random mating and large size.

Use it to detect that evolution is happening: if observed frequencies deviate from Hardy–Weinberg
expectation, one of the assumptions is violated. It is a null model, and its value is diagnostic.

## Speciation

### Allopatric

Geographic separation → independent mutation and selection → reproductive isolation. The standard
model, and the only one that is easy to demonstrate.

### Sympatric

Speciation without geographic separation, via polyploidy (common in plants), habitat
differentiation, or sexual selection. Rarer in animals, well documented in plants.

### Isolating mechanisms

- **Prezygotic** — habitat, temporal, behavioural, mechanical, gametic isolation.
- **Postzygotic** — hybrid inviability, hybrid sterility, hybrid breakdown.

Speciation is complete when the two populations could not produce fertile offspring if reunited.

### Rates

**Gradualism** versus **punctuated equilibrium**. The evidence supports both operating at
different timescales and in different lineages; the dichotomy is a useful frame, not a strict
either/or.

### Mass extinctions

Five major events. Each removed a large fraction of species and created ecological opportunity for
survivors. Adaptive radiation following extinction is one of the clearest patterns in the fossil
record — and the context in which current extinction rates should be read.

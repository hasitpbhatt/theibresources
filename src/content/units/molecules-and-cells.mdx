## Water and biological molecules

### Why water is the solvent of life

- **Polarity** — uneven charge distribution makes it dissolve ions and polar molecules.
- **Hydrogen bonding** — gives high specific heat capacity (thermal buffer), high latent heat of
  vaporisation (cooling by sweating and transpiration), and high surface tension.
- **Cohesion and adhesion** — enables capillary action in xylem.
- **Density anomaly** — ice floats, insulating water below and allowing life to persist through
  winter.

Every one of those is a physical consequence of hydrogen bonding. Learn the mechanism once and
the list becomes derivable rather than memorised.

### Carbohydrates

Monosaccharides (glucose, fructose), disaccharides (sucrose, lactose, maltose), polysaccharides
(starch, glycogen, cellulose, chitin).

- **Starch** — α-glucose, branched (amylopectin) or helical (amylose). Storage in plants. Compact,
  insoluble, easily hydrolysed.
- **Glycogen** — α-glucose, more heavily branched than starch. Storage in animals. Faster
  mobilisation because of more free ends.
- **Cellulose** — β-glucose, forming straight chains with alternating flips, cross-linked by
  hydrogen bonds into microfibrils. Structural. Indigestible to most animals because they lack
  cellulase.

The α/β distinction is the whole story: one bond geometry separates lunch from lumber.

### Lipids

Triglycerides (glycerol + three fatty acids) and phospholipids (glycerol + two fatty acids +
phosphate group).

- **Saturated** fatty acids have no C=C bonds → straight chains → pack tightly → solid at room
  temperature.
- **Unsaturated** have C=C bonds → kinks → cannot pack → liquid at room temperature.

Functions: energy storage (more than double the energy per gram of carbohydrate, because they
are more reduced), insulation, membrane structure, hormones, waterproofing.

### Proteins

Amino acids join by **peptide bonds** (condensation between the amine group of one and the
carboxyl group of the next). The sequence is the **primary structure**.

- **Secondary** — α-helix and β-pleated sheet, held by hydrogen bonds along the backbone.
- **Tertiary** — overall 3D shape, held by hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, disulphide bridges,
  hydrophobic interactions.
- **Quaternary** — multiple polypeptide chains assembled (haemoglobin: four).

**Denaturation** destroys secondary and tertiary structure without breaking peptide bonds. That
distinction matters: a denatured protein is not a hydrolysed protein.

### Nucleic acids

DNA and RNA are polymers of nucleotides, each with a pentose sugar, a phosphate group and a
nitrogenous base.

- DNA: deoxyribose, double helix, A–T and G–C, hydrogen bonded, antiparallel.
- RNA: ribose, usually single-stranded, A–U instead of A–T.

Base pairing explains replication fidelity: each strand is a template for the other.

## Cell structure

### Prokaryotic and eukaryotic

| Feature | Prokaryote | Eukaryote |
|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | Absent | Present |
| DNA | Circular, naked | Linear, associated with histones |
| Ribosomes | 70S | 80S |
| Membrane-bound organelles | Absent | Present |
| Size | 0.5–5 µm | 10–100 µm |

### Organelles worth knowing properly

- **Nucleus** — chromatin, nucleolus (ribosome subunit assembly), nuclear envelope with pores.
- **Mitochondrion** — double membrane, cristae (surface area for the electron transport chain),
  matrix (Krebs cycle), own DNA.
- **Chloroplast** — double membrane, thylakoids stacked into grana (light-dependent reactions),
  stroma (light-independent reactions), own DNA.
- **Rough ER** — ribosome-studded, protein synthesis and folding.
- **Smooth ER** — lipid synthesis, detoxification.
- **Golgi** — modification, sorting, packaging.
- **Lysosome** — hydrolytic enzymes, low internal pH.
- **Ribosome** — translation; free in cytoplasm or bound to ER.
- **Vacuole** — storage, turgor, and in plants a major contributor to cell volume.

The mitochondrion and chloroplast having their own DNA is the strongest single piece of evidence
for the endosymbiotic theory.

## Membranes and transport

### The fluid mosaic model

A phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins, cholesterol, glycolipids and glycoproteins, all
mobile within the plane of the membrane.

Cholesterol modulates fluidity: it reduces fluidity at high temperature and prevents packing at
low temperature. It is a buffer, not simply a stiffener.

### Transport mechanisms

| Mechanism | Direction | Energy | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple diffusion | Down gradient | None | O₂, CO₂ |
| Facilitated diffusion | Down gradient, via protein | None | Glucose via GLUT |
| Osmosis | Down water potential gradient | None | Water through aquaporins |
| Active transport | Against gradient | ATP | Na⁺/K⁺ pump |
| Endocytosis / exocytosis | Bulk | ATP | Phagocytosis, secretion |

**Water potential** (Ψ) is the tendency of water to move. Pure water at atmospheric pressure has
Ψ = 0; solutes lower it, so solutions have negative Ψ. Water moves from higher Ψ to lower Ψ.

This is why a plant cell in pure water becomes turgid (cell wall resists further entry) and an
animal cell bursts — the wall is the difference between a mechanism and a catastrophe.

### Surface area to volume ratio

As a cell grows, volume increases as the cube while surface area increases as the square, so SA:V
falls. Diffusion across the membrane cannot keep pace with metabolic demand.

Every adaptation for exchange is a solution to this ratio problem: flattening, folding, branching,
or dividing. Alveoli, villi, gill lamellae, root hairs, cristae, and cell division itself are all
the same geometric fix applied to different problems.

## Enzymes and metabolism

### Mechanism

Enzymes lower activation energy by stabilising the transition state. The **induced fit** model
describes the active site changing shape as the substrate binds.

### Factors

- **Temperature** — rate rises with kinetic energy until denaturation. The optimum is a
  compromise between speed and stability.
- **pH** — extremes alter charge on the active site, disrupting binding.
- **Substrate concentration** — rate rises then plateaus when all active sites are occupied
  (Vmax).
- **Enzyme concentration** — proportional if substrate is not limiting.

### Inhibition

- **Competitive** — inhibitor resembles substrate, binds the active site. Raises apparent Km,
  Vmax unchanged, overcome by more substrate.
- **Non-competitive** — binds elsewhere, changes active site shape. Lowers Vmax, Km unchanged,
  not overcome by more substrate.

### Metabolic pathways

Anabolism builds (endergonic, requires energy); catabolism breaks down (exergonic, releases
energy). Pathways are regulated by feedback inhibition, usually on the first enzyme, which is the
most efficient place to intervene.

## Cell division

### Mitosis

Prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase, then cytokinesis. Produces two genetically identical
diploid cells. Purpose: growth, repair, asexual reproduction.

### Meiosis

Two divisions, four haploid cells, each genetically unique. Purpose: gamete production.

Sources of variation:

1. **Crossing over** in prophase I — exchange between homologous chromosomes.
2. **Independent assortment** in metaphase I — random orientation of homologues.
3. **Random fertilisation** — which gametes meet.

### Cancer

Uncontrolled division following mutation in genes controlling the cell cycle. Oncogenes promote
division; tumour suppressor genes restrain it. Both brakes and accelerators must fail.

The **cell cycle checkpoints** are the reason most mutations do not become cancer: damaged cells
are arrested or destroyed. Cancer requires escaping those checkpoints, not just a mutation.

## Soil formation

Soil is rock broken down and colonised. Formation takes five interacting factors, often
remembered as **CLORPT**:

- **Climate** — temperature and moisture drive weathering and biological activity.
- **Organisms** — roots, microbes, worms, fungi do most of the mixing.
- **Relief** — slope angle controls drainage and erosion.
- **Parent material** — the mineral starting point.
- **Time** — centuries to millennia for a mature profile.

Soil formation rates are roughly 0.01–0.1 mm per year in most systems. Erosion rates under
poor management can exceed that by two or three orders of magnitude. That arithmetic is the
entire soil crisis in one line.

### Weathering

- **Physical**: freeze–thaw, heating and cooling, root pressure, abrasion.
- **Chemical**: hydrolysis, oxidation, carbonation, solution.
- **Biological**: organic acids from roots and lichens, burrowing, mixing.

Physical weathering creates surface area; chemical weathering changes what the surface is.
Both are needed for a real soil.

## Texture and structure

### Texture

The mineral fraction is classified by particle size:

| Class | Diameter | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Sand | 0.05–2 mm | Drains fast, low water holding |
| Silt | 0.002–0.05 mm | Intermediate |
| Clay | < 0.002 mm | Holds water and nutrients, drains slowly |

**Loam** is a balanced mixture and is generally the most workable agricultural soil. Texture
cannot be changed practically — you inherit it.

### Structure

Structure is how particles clump into aggregates. Good structure means crumbs and pores;
poor structure means a compacted mass. Unlike texture, structure **can** be improved or
destroyed by management.

Pore space matters more than total volume: macropores drain and aerate, micropores hold
water. A compacted soil loses macropores first, which is why compaction causes both runoff and
drought stress simultaneously.

## Soil profiles

A vertical section typically shows horizons:

- **O** — organic litter on the surface.
- **A** — topsoil, dark, root-dense, biologically active.
- **E** — eluvial zone, leached of clay and iron (in podzols).
- **B** — subsoil, accumulation of clay, iron, or carbonate.
- **C** — weathered parent material.
- **R** — bedrock.

Reading a profile tells you about drainage (mottling means waterlogging), leaching (pale E
horizon means strong downward flow), and history (buried horizons mean deposition or
disturbance).

## Fertility and nutrients

Fertility is not the same as nutrient content. A fertile soil holds nutrients in available
form, retains water, has good structure, supports a healthy microbial community, and has
workable pH.

### Cation exchange capacity

Clay and organic matter carry negative charges that hold positively charged nutrients (Ca²⁺,
Mg²⁺, K⁺, NH₄⁺). This is **cation exchange capacity (CEC)**. High CEC means the soil holds
nutrients against leaching; low CEC means nutrients wash straight through.

Sandy soils have low CEC. That is why they need frequent, small fertiliser applications rather
than one large one.

### pH

pH controls nutrient availability more than any other single variable. Most nutrients are most
available near pH 6.5. Acidification locks up phosphorus and mobilises aluminium; alkalinity
locks up iron, manganese and zinc.

## Erosion and degradation

### Water erosion

Splash, sheet, rill, gully. The progression matters: sheet erosion is nearly invisible and
removes the most fertile fraction first, so yield declines before anyone notices soil leaving.

### Wind erosion

Suspension (fine particles travel far), saltation (bouncing mid-size grains, the main mechanism),
and creep (large grains rolling). Dry, bare, fine-textured surfaces are most vulnerable.

### Control

- **Contour ploughing and terracing** — reduce slope length.
- **Cover crops and mulching** — protect the surface.
- **Windbreaks** — reduce surface wind speed.
- **Reduced tillage** — preserve structure and residue.
- **Organic matter addition** — improve aggregation.

All of these work by either reducing the energy of the eroding agent or increasing the soil's
resistance. Every technique is one or the other.

### Other degradation

- **Salinisation** — irrigation without drainage concentrates salts at the surface.
- **Waterlogging** — rising water tables saturate the root zone.
- **Compaction** — machinery and grazing destroy macropores.
- **Contamination** — heavy metals, persistent organics, excess nutrients.
- **Sealing** — paving removes soil from function entirely.

Soil is effectively a non-renewable resource on human timescales. Treat the loss as permanent.
